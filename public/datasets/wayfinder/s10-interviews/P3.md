# Session 10 interview snapshot: P3

Fictional practice data written for the AI-native PM academy. Wayfinder is not a real product, and all participants, venues, transit routes and figures are invented.

## Session metadata

- Participant: `P3` (pseudonymised; real name and employer omitted)
- Consent: verbal consent recorded for written notes and pseudonymised synthesis with Claude; no audio recording made
- Trip rhythm: 5 to 6 luxury long weekends a year staying at five-star boutique hotels (`$1,200` per day budget for two), relying on the hotel concierge desk for private transfers, restaurant tables and guided museum entry
- Setup: confirmation emails forwarded to hotel concierge; no shared budget sheet or transit planning app
- Format: 30-minute video call (15 minutes story interview, 15 minutes static probe test on mobile)

## Part 1: Story interview (00:00 to 15:00)

- **Context and weekend routine:** Picks a city around a fine-dining reservation or gallery opening, emails the hotel concierge two weeks ahead with three things they want to see, and lets the concierge arrange private cars and timed tickets.
- **Asked: walk me through the last specific time a closed venue, transit delay or group budget split caused friction on a weekend trip.**
  - Ten-second pause while thinking.
  - Quote: "Honestly, I am struggling to remember one. In Vienna last autumn it started sleeting while we were at an exhibition, so we messaged the hotel concierge on WhatsApp, they moved our afternoon walking tour indoors to a private palace collection and sent a car in ten minutes."
  - On budget tracking: "We don't really set a weekend per-person cap. As long as the hotel and restaurants are worth it, whether Saturday activities cost $90.00 or $450.00 doesn't change what we do."
- **Current workaround:** None. No notes checklist, no spreadsheet, no public-transit buffer lookups.
- **Feature wish / tangent (minutes 10 to 15, ~5 minutes):**
  - Quote: "What I would pay $25.00 a month for is a cancellation sniper for two-Michelin-star tasting menus that grabs a 20:00 Friday table the second someone drops out, plus tracking which of our airport lounge passes include champagne dining."

## Part 2: Static probe test (15:00 to 30:00)

Shown the Level 2 static HTML discovery probe `Day Plan & Budget Split` on mobile (`Per-person cap: $180.00`; 3 booked stops totalling `$94.50`; `$85.50` with caption `Left after booked stops (meals and local transit not included)`; `16:00 ferry transfer clash` warning; no booking or hold CTA on screen).

- **Task and spontaneous praise (polite compliment trap):**
  - Scanned the screen in about ten seconds and smiled warmly.
  - Quote: "Oh, the layout and typography look super clean! Very calm spacing, way prettier than clunky travel spreadsheets. I would totally download this for our October weekend in Rome, I'd probably use it every day!" _(Observer note: directly contradicts Part 1 behaviour where `P3` delegates all logistics and rerouting to a five-star hotel concierge and never tracks a `$180.00` activity budget or tram times.)_
- **Comprehension check ("What is included in `$85.50`, and what is the `16:00` warning telling you?"):**
  - Answered both accurately: "$180.00 cap minus $94.50 of booked activities leaves $85.50 before meals and local transport, and the 16:00 banner says the tram from Tile Museum takes 42 minutes so you'll miss kayak check-in."
  - Spent the remaining six minutes suggesting aesthetic polish (editorial serif headings, sommelier wine-list tags and private chauffeur time estimates instead of tram buffers).
